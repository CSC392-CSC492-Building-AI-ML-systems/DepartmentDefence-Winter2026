Fixed time/unit rate This basis of payment reimburses the contractor for the actual hours worked or other units procured in performance of the work. The time/units can represent labour hours or machine hours. The amount paid for these hours or other units is calculated based on a predetermined fixed rate, typically composed of estimated direct costs, indirect costs (overhead charges) and profit. This payment method ensures the contractor is paid for the actual work done at a pre-agreed rate while allowing for financial oversight through audits and adherence to negotiated limits. Conditions for using fixed time/unit rate Use this basis of payment when the level of effort and/or quantities required to perform the contract cannot be estimated in advance, but the applicable direct and indirect costs for each hour worked or other unit procured during the contract period can be determined. You must include adequate cost controls in the contract to prevent inefficient or wasteful methods by the contractor. The government may audit the actual hours worked or units spent in performance of the work. You must include audit clause for time verification and rate certifications. Negotiating fixed time/unit rate For non-competitive contracts, the fixed rate should be negotiated between the contractor and you, the buyer, with support from the Procurement Support Service Sector (PSSS) price advisors . You must validate price and technical details prior to contract award to ensure the cost estimates are reasonable and that the contractor’s financial systems supporting the estimates are sufficient and reliable. Incorporating contractual limitations Use one the following clauses with fixed time/unit rate basis of payment: Ceiling price If included, the contractor must complete the prescribed work without additional payment, even if actual costs exceed the ceiling price. When used, there must be full agreement with all parties as to what constitutes the prescribed work. Limitation of expenditure Without a ceiling price, you must include a limitation of expenditure in the contract. A limitation of expenditure set the maximum amount the contractor may be paid for the prescribed work. This is typically used when the level of effort is uncertain. At the client’s request, you will amend the contact to provide additional funds, or request the contractor to complete the work within the limits of that the original funding. To learn more about the fixed time/unit rate basis of payment refer to section 4.1.2 of the Practitioner’s Guide for Procurement Pricing (PDF, 6.50 MB).
