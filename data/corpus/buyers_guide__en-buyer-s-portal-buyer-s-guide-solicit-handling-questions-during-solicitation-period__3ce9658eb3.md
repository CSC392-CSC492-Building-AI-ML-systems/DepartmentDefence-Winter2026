Handling questions during the solicitation period Businesses should submit any questions in writing to the buyer before the deadline stated in the solicitation document. In responding to queries, you should take care to protect the identity of businesses submitting questions. For a simple enquiry, you may provide the answer directly to the business asking the question. This is only if the answer will not affect other businesses or how they will respond to the solicitation. More complex or technical questions concerning the requirement itself should be forwarded to the client for response back to the buyer, to ensure that clear and accurate responses are provided. You should consolidate all questions and answers into an addendum/amendment to the solicitation, and then: publish the addendum/amendment on the Government Electronic Tendering Service (GETS), if publicly advertised issue the addendum or amendment directly to businesses (for solicitations not publicly advertised) Questions must be answered robustly to give the maximum practical amount of context to businesses. While it is the client's responsibility to draft these answers, you should challenge the supplied answers when there are insufficient details or clarity. Changes to the solicitation itself to reflect clarifications resulting from the questions, including extensions to the solicitation period, if granted, must be formally incorporated through an amendment to the solicitation. Businesses are responsible for monitoring the GETS for any updates or amendments to the solicitation notices and documents.

## Handling questions during the solicitation period

Businesses should submit any questions in writing to the buyer before the deadline stated in the solicitation document.

In responding to queries, you should take care to protect the identity of businesses submitting questions.

For a simple enquiry, you may provide the answer directly to the business asking the question. This is only if the answer will not affect other businesses or how they will respond to the solicitation.

More complex or technical questions concerning the requirement itself should be forwarded to the client for response back to the buyer, to ensure that clear and accurate responses are provided. You should consolidate all questions and answers into an addendum/amendment to the solicitation, and then:

- publish the addendum/amendment on the Government Electronic Tendering Service (GETS), if publicly advertised
- issue the addendum or amendment directly to businesses (for solicitations not publicly advertised)

Changes to the solicitation itself to reflect clarifications resulting from the questions, including extensions to the solicitation period, if granted, must be formally incorporated through an amendment to the solicitation.

Businesses are responsible for monitoring the GETS for any updates or amendments to the solicitation notices and documents.
