Determine the sourcing strategy When deciding on a sourcing strategy for procurement you should evaluate the following three key factors: method of supply; sourcing approach; and advertising strategy. Specifically, you should determine whether an existing method of supply should be used. Decide if the sourcing should be competitive or non-competitive and identify the most effective way to advertise. Whenever possible, contractors must be selected using a competitive process. Your flexibility to depart from this approach and the type of competitive solicitation that may be used will depend on the procurement framework in place. Tendering approaches under the trade agreements There are three tendering approaches under trade agreements: open, selective, and limited. Each method has distinct procedural requirements to ensure transparency, fairness and compliance with the trade agreements. Open tendering This approach is characterized by you publishing a notice of proposed procurement (NPP) to invite all interested businesses to submit offers. Open tendering is the preferred approach. Selective tendering This approach involves either a one-time list or a permanent list of prequalified offerors. You must allow all prequalified offerors to participate in a procurement, unless the procuring entity specifies in its prequalification request any limitation on the number of offerors permitted to submit an offer and the criteria for that strategy. One-time list of prequalified offerors This is a two-stage process where you start by publishing an NPP to identify potential interested businesses who meet predetermined qualifications for participation. Then, you issue tender documentation to those prequalified offerors. For procurements subject to one or more international trade agreements, you must publish an NPP at both stages. It is also acceptable to amend the original NPP once the tender closing date has been determined. Permanent list of prequalified offerors This approach involves publishing an NPP to identify potential interested businesses that meet predetermined qualifications for participation. Next, you will develop a list of prequalified offerors, which will be maintained and used for several different individual solicitation processes. Tender documents are issued for individual solicitations under this list. Any other potential offeror who requests tender documentation must be considered, as long as there is sufficient time to carry out the prequalification process. You must publish an annual notice of the list's existence on the tender opportunities page, if covered by the Canadian Free Trade Agreement (CFTA) or one or more international trade agreements. At PSPC , obligations regarding permanent lists of prequalified offerors are operationalized as “supply arrangements.” A permanent list of qualified offerors may also be referred to as a “source list” and is referred to in many trade agreements as a “multi-use list.” Limited tendering This approach allows you to move away from certain procedural obligations of the applicable trade agreement(s) under specific circumstances, for example, if you receive no suitable offers from open or selective tendering. You should not use limited tendering to avoid competition, to discriminate against any business, or to protect domestic businesses. It must comply with the trade agreements, particularly in technical specifications. Typically, limited tendering is competitive, but it can be non-competitive or used for sole sourcing when justified. You are advised to adhere closely to trade agreement obligations, deviating from procedural obligations only as necessary and permitted. For procurements covered by multiple trade agreements, the limited tendering provision of each agreement must contain a valid justification. It should also refer to the procedural obligations from which you may choose to deviate. Low dollar value Procurements that do not exceed $25,000 for goods or $40,000 for services and construction, inclusive of all taxes, are considered to be low dollar value (LDV) procurements. These procurements are carried out following the PSPC guidelines specified in the Directive on the Processing of Low Dollar Value Procurements (PDF, 27 KB, accessible only on the Government of Canada network). When selecting a contractor, you should use the most efficient and cost-effective method. This might involve soliciting offers, or directly selecting a sole offeror when soliciting offers is not cost-effective. You are tasked with choosing the most suitable procurement strategy for each LDV procurement. The aim is to obtain the best value while ensuring promptness and cost effectiveness in alignment with PSPC ’s guiding principles. Additionally, you must complete a risk assessment and document the procurement file. This must include the rationale behind the chosen procurement strategy and explain how the estimated contract value was determined. Non-competitive When offers are not solicited, you must ensure that the client thoroughly justifies the use of any exception to the competitive solicitation. They need to cite the relevant exception under the Government Contracts Regulations (GCRs) of the Financial Administration Act (FAA) and considering the limited tendering provisions of Canada's trade agreements. You must also factor in the procurement provisions of the CLCA s. The GCRs generally require the solicitation of offers before entering a contract. A contract may be formed without soliciting offers when: The need is of pressing emergency that threatens public interest ( GCRs 6.(a)), such as actual or imminent life-threatening situations, disasters endangering quality of life or resulting in loss of life, or events causing significant damage to Crown property The estimated expenditure does not exceed: $25,000 for goods and $40,000 for services and construction contracts $100,000 for contracts involving architectural, engineering and other services related to the planning, design, preparation or supervision of construction, repair, renovation or restoration projects $100,000 for contracts entered into by the Minister of International Development involving architectural, engineering, or other services related to the planning, design, preparation or supervision of an international development assistance program or project ( GCRs 6.(b)) Soliciting offers would not serve the public interest due to the nature of the work ( GCRs 6.(c)) Only one entity is capable of performing the contract due to unique requirements, such as patents, copyrights, technical compatibility or specialised technological expertise ( GCRs 6.(d)) For procurements above $25,000 for goods and $40,000 for services and construction, greater diligence is required when invoking the exception where only one entity is capable of performing the work. In such cases, you must answer the TB questions for sole source , with support from the client. These responses should be included in the approval documents and procurement file. For procurements subject to trade agreements, you may award contracts without soliciting offers only if they align with one of the limited tendering reasons outlined in the applicable trade agreement. Employing limited tendering does not always mean a non-competitive process. Justification of non-competitive process Clients must clearly justify the rationale for any exception to soliciting offers. You are responsible for ensuring the rationale is adequately supported and you must also consider the procurement provisions under the CLCA s. If provided rationale is inadequate or if there is no supporting information, you are to guide the client towards alternative products or sources. The goal is to agree on the most suitable procurement strategy. If you are unable to reach a consensus with the client, escalate the matter to higher management. If you use any of the GCRs exceptions, you must include documentation detailing the procurement strategy and the reasoning for the selected exception. Additionally, when engaging in limited tendering under trade agreements, you must document the specific reasons and relevant articles justifying this approach. When issuing non-competitive standing offers, you must provide a rationale to justify using this process. If a standing offer is issued using a non-competitive procurement strategy or does not have a clear ranking methodology, resulting call-ups are considered non-competitive. In these situations, clients must provide the non-competitive rationale following the guidelines mentioned above. For example, if a non-competitive standing offer is issued to ABC Inc. with a sole source justification that the offeror has sole intellectual property (IP) rights for a unique scientific process, every subsequent call-up will require a distinct sole source justification. This justification must clearly demonstrate why ABC Inc.’s unique scientific process is the only acceptable method to meet the requirement and, if applicable, why other similar scientific processes would not be acceptable. ### Tendering approaches under the trade agreements

There are three tendering approaches under trade agreements: open, selective, and limited. Each method has distinct procedural requirements to ensure transparency, fairness and compliance with the trade agreements.

#### Open tendering

This approach is characterized by you publishing a notice of proposed procurement (NPP) to invite all interested businesses to submit offers. Open tendering is the preferred approach.

#### Selective tendering

This approach involves either a one-time list or a permanent list of prequalified offerors. You must allow all prequalified offerors to participate in a procurement, unless the procuring entity specifies in its prequalification request any limitation on the number of offerors permitted to submit an offer and the criteria for that strategy.

##### One-time list of prequalified offerors

This is a two-stage process where you start by publishing an NPP to identify potential interested businesses who meet predetermined qualifications for participation. Then, you issue tender documentation to those prequalified offerors. For procurements subject to one or more international trade agreements, you must publish an NPP at both stages. It is also acceptable to amend the original NPP once the tender closing date has been determined.

##### Permanent list of prequalified offerors

This approach involves publishing an NPP to identify potential interested businesses that meet predetermined qualifications for participation.

Next, you will develop a list of prequalified offerors, which will be maintained and used for several different individual solicitation processes. Tender documents are issued for individual solicitations under this list.

Any other potential offeror who requests tender documentation must be considered, as long as there is sufficient time to carry out the prequalification process.

You must publish an annual notice of the list's existence on the tender opportunities page, if covered by the Canadian Free Trade Agreement (CFTA) or one or more international trade agreements.

At PSPC , obligations regarding permanent lists of prequalified offerors are operationalized as “supply arrangements.” A permanent list of qualified offerors may also be referred to as a “source list” and is referred to in many trade agreements as a “multi-use list.”

This approach allows you to move away from certain procedural obligations of the applicable trade agreement(s) under specific circumstances, for example, if you receive no suitable offers from open or selective tendering.

You should not use limited tendering to avoid competition, to discriminate against any business, or to protect domestic businesses. It must comply with the trade agreements, particularly in technical specifications. Typically, limited tendering is competitive, but it can be non-competitive or used for sole sourcing when justified. You are advised to adhere closely to trade agreement obligations, deviating from procedural obligations only as necessary and permitted.

For procurements covered by multiple trade agreements, the limited tendering provision of each agreement must contain a valid justification. It should also refer to the procedural obligations from which you may choose to deviate.

In general, by complying with the obligations specified in the World Trade Organization – Agreement on Government Procurement (WTO-AGP) and Canada-European Union Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA), you will meet the requirements for the other trade agreements. Nonetheless, for clarity and compliance, referencing the specific limited tendering provisions in the applicable agreements is recommended.

Additionally, you must complete a risk assessment and document the procurement file. This must include the rationale behind the chosen procurement strategy and explain how the estimated contract value was determined.

There are many tools available for identifying and selecting offerors, such as:

- Supplier Registration Information (SRI) system
- Tender opportunities page
- Telephone and online trade directories

These tools support identifying and selecting offerors through competitive or directed means, enhancing efficiency and accessibility in procurement processes.

### Non-competitive

When offers are not solicited, you must ensure that the client thoroughly justifies the use of any exception to the competitive solicitation. They need to cite the relevant exception under the Government Contracts Regulations (GCRs) of the Financial Administration Act (FAA) and considering the limited tendering provisions of Canada's trade agreements. You must also factor in the procurement provisions of the CLCA s.

The GCRs generally require the solicitation of offers before entering a contract.

A contract may be formed without soliciting offers when:

- The need is of pressing emergency that threatens public interest ( GCRs 6.(a)), such as actual or imminent life-threatening situations, disasters endangering quality of life or resulting in loss of life, or events causing significant damage to Crown property
- The estimated expenditure does not exceed:
- $25,000 for goods and $40,000 for services and construction contracts
- $100,000 for contracts involving architectural, engineering and other services related to the planning, design, preparation or supervision of construction, repair, renovation or restoration projects
- $100,000 for contracts entered into by the Minister of International Development involving architectural, engineering, or other services related to the planning, design, preparation or supervision of an international development assistance program or project ( GCRs 6.(b))
- Soliciting offers would not serve the public interest due to the nature of the work ( GCRs 6.(c))
- Only one entity is capable of performing the contract due to unique requirements, such as patents, copyrights, technical compatibility or specialised technological expertise ( GCRs 6.(d))

For procurements above $25,000 for goods and $40,000 for services and construction, greater diligence is required when invoking the exception where only one entity is capable of performing the work. In such cases, you must answer the TB questions for sole source , with support from the client. These responses should be included in the approval documents and procurement file.

For procurements subject to trade agreements, you may award contracts without soliciting offers only if they align with one of the limited tendering reasons outlined in the applicable trade agreement. Employing limited tendering does not always mean a non-competitive process.

#### Justification of non-competitive process

Clients must clearly justify the rationale for any exception to soliciting offers. You are responsible for ensuring the rationale is adequately supported and you must also consider the procurement provisions under the CLCA s.

If provided rationale is inadequate or if there is no supporting information, you are to guide the client towards alternative products or sources. The goal is to agree on the most suitable procurement strategy. If you are unable to reach a consensus with the client, escalate the matter to higher management.

If you use any of the GCRs exceptions, you must include documentation detailing the procurement strategy and the reasoning for the selected exception. Additionally, when engaging in limited tendering under trade agreements, you must document the specific reasons and relevant articles justifying this approach.

When issuing non-competitive standing offers, you must provide a rationale to justify using this process. If a standing offer is issued using a non-competitive procurement strategy or does not have a clear ranking methodology, resulting call-ups are considered non-competitive. In these situations, clients must provide the non-competitive rationale following the guidelines mentioned above.

For example, if a non-competitive standing offer is issued to ABC Inc. with a sole source justification that the offeror has sole intellectual property (IP) rights for a unique scientific process, every subsequent call-up will require a distinct sole source justification. This justification must clearly demonstrate why ABC Inc.’s unique scientific process is the only acceptable method to meet the requirement and, if applicable, why other similar scientific processes would not be acceptable.

This process is designed to thoroughly justify and explain the decision. You must consider all questions and prepare responses with the client. You must address each question and confirm non-applicability where necessary, to clearly outline the rationale behind the sole source selection.

For contracts or standing offers for services, you are tasked with confirming that the procurement method chosen is the most suitable for the requirement. Methods include, but are not limited to, grants, contributions or various forms of employment contracts for example a term, a casual or ministerial appointment..

### Use a source list

You should consider the following points when using a source list.

#### Direct invitation

Source lists primarily serve to request offers from offerors when a competitive procurement process isn't publicly advertised.

When using source lists instead of rotational source lists, you can:

- Include any business that requests to participate and such businesses must be considered for evaluation
- Augment these lists with your knowledge of potential businesses and client recommendations, enriching the pool of candidates and potentially enhancing procurement outcomes

#### Use a source list under the trade agreements

For procurement requirements subject to the international trade agreements and the CFTA , source lists are useful for establishing a list of pre-qualified offerors of specific goods and services. They are managed through:

- Annual updates:
- You should refresh source lists at least annually, to maintain current and competitive offeror options
- Open tendering:
- You must use open tendering procedures using the Government Electronic Tendering Service (GETS) to invite businesses to submit their expressions of qualifications for evaluation and placement on the list, if they meet the selection criteria
- Businesses must be allowed to qualify at any time, and all who meet the selection criteria must be included on the pre-qualified list

#### Specific considerations under the Canadian Free Trade Agreement

For a solicitation of offers that is subject to the CFTA only, you must invite all pre-qualified offerors on the source list to submit offers. You must do this unless the pre-qualification invitation stated that there would be a limitation on the number of pre-qualified offerors allowed to submit offers. The pre-qualification invitation must also have included the criteria for selecting that limited number of pre-qualified offerors. In this case, you may limit the number of invited pre-qualified offerors, and you are not required to post an NPP on GETS .

#### Compliance with international trade agreements

Notice of proposed procurement (NPP): For solicitations that are subject to one or more international trade agreements you must publish an NPP on the Tender opportunities page. All pre-qualified offerors on the source list must be able to submit an offer.

Inclusion of new businesses: You must allow businesses not initially on the source list to apply for pre-qualification upon request. Any businesses that qualify must be integrated into the source list in a timely manner and be allowed to participate in the procurement in question.

#### Requirements not subject to trade agreements

For procurement needs not subject to trade agreements, where open competition is not appropriate you should use source lists in the ways listed below.

Use direct solicitation: You must solicit offers directly from a pre-qualified list of offerors for specific goods or services. If no such list exists, use the Supplier Registration Information service to identify potential offerors, particularly for low dollar value procurements.

Include business recommendations: You may incorporate businesses recommended by the client into the source list to broaden potential sourcing options.

##### Automated source lists

You must use an automated source list system, such as the Automated Vendor Rotation System (AVRS), as applicable, to ensure systematic rotation of and equitable opportunities for all offerors.

##### Opportunity to participate

You must allow any offeror expressing interest on a specific requirement to submit an offer, except when it requires cancelling an existing solicitation of offers. This does not generally apply to rotational source lists, which may restrict participation to pre-qualified offerors.

##### Best value and fairness

You should make an effort to ensure best value to Canada in terms of who is invited. Ensure the principles of "fairness and access" are displayed in a practical manner by rotating opportunities to submit an offer within the offerors on any given list.

#### Identification of and source lists for Indigenous businesses

Use internal systems to identify Indigenous businesses who have self-declared as being Indigenous. These systems help compile comprehensive source lists of potential Indigenous businesses, applicable across all commodities—goods, services or construction.

#### Solicitation process for Indigenous set-asides

Always consider issuing a solicitation for a standing offer or a supply arrangement specifically for a Procurement Strategy for Indigenous Business (PSIB) set-aside stream. This allows clients the possibility of contracting with Indigenous firms if they wish to do a set-aside procurement under PSIB , particularly for mandatory commodities. This approach enables clients to engage with Indigenous businesses under set-aside procurement initiatives.

#### Additional resources for identifying Indigenous businesses

Access the Indigenous Services Canada’s Indigenous Business Directory for more information on Indigenous businesses and to identify potential Indigenous businesses for procurement opportunities.

If you have questions about the directory, contact the Business Development Directorate , Indigenous Services Canada (ISC), directly.
