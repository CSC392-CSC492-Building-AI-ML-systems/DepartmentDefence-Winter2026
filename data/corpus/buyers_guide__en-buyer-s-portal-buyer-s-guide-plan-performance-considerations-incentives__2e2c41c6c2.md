Incentives Incentives are tools used in contracting, designed to maximize value for both Canada and the contractor. This is achieved by motivating and rewarding the achievement of Canada’s desired outcomes, when appropriate. Incentives can be both short-term to achieve specific goals, or long-term to encourage the sustainment of behaviours. For additional guidance on incentives, see the Practitioner’s Guide for Procurement Pricing . The effectiveness and appropriateness of incentives can vary based on the contract type and the contractor's motivation and ability to meet contract performance criteria. Incentives are structured to be challenging yet achievable, aligning closely with procurement objectives without rewarding minimal or expected performance levels. Incentives may not be suitable for all contracts. If the contractor would achieve the target performance criteria without incentives, it is unlikely to be motivated by them. If there is little value to Canada for contract performance to exceed the base standards. Incentives should align with key procurement objectives and be balanced to avoid merely rewarding the meeting of contract requirements. They should be challenging yet feasible, avoiding overly simple targets that add little value or overly difficult ones that might discourage a contractor to invest the effort. Avoid the use of too many incentives in a single contract. See the section on Multiple Incentives and Competing Objectives in section 4.4 Incentives of the Practitioner’s Guide for Procurement Pricing . Competitive contracts Introduce incentives in the solicitation of offers, as part of the resulting contract clauses. If there are uncertainties in terms of the application of incentives, specific incentive parameters can be stated in the solicitation. Non-competitive contracts Introduce incentives during price negotiations, for application anytime throughout the life of the contract. Consider the extent of control that the contractor has over the achievement of the incentives, and if the contractor’s systems can track the required performance metrics. Assess that the benefits of achieving contract outcomes outweigh the costs of the incentives and their administration to all parties. Basis of payment Incentives can be used with all basis of payment types to reward superior contract performance which exceeds the base standards established in the statement of requirement/work and provides value to Canada. Strategic considerations Keep incentive structures simple with high-level objective measurements rather than complex formulas. Simplify their administration. Any applicable economic price adjustment or foreign currency adjustment must be excluded from incentive calculations. Engage all relevant stakeholders (such as program, technical, acquisition and price advisors) in the incentive planning process. Modelling of the impact and value of incentive programs to Canada and the contractor is essential. An incentive plan that is not informed by all parties and perspectives can result in unintended consequences on a procurement. This includes any profit or base-fee, upon an evaluation of performance against pre-established criteria. Application : appropriate when the performance aspects are difficult to quantify, and the contract's scope, complexity, dollar value and expected benefits justify the administrative effort involved in managing the award fees. This is particularly relevant when evaluating performance that is subjective in nature and it is not feasible to determine objective incentive targes applicable to cost, schedule or technical performance. Implementation plan : requires a detailed plan that outlines the evaluation structure, and aligns with Canada's strategy, goals and objectives for the procurement. This plan must articulate the award fee amount, and evaluation details such as evaluation periods, teams, categories, criteria, ratings and process. Fees may be assessed during a single evaluation period or over multiple periods. Evaluation methodology : an evaluation team with the appropriate technical and procurement expertise should be established. Develop performance categories with point or score ranges for each criterion. Total scores for each category are based on points earned per criterion. Evaluation process : establish and document a clear process to collect and review performance data. This includes setting evaluation periods, defining how data will be collected, frequency of data collection and establishing a communication protocol for the evaluation process and sharing results.

## Incentives

### Application of incentives

The application of incentives vary depending on whether the contract is competitive or not.

#### Competitive contracts

Introduce incentives in the solicitation of offers, as part of the resulting contract clauses. If there are uncertainties in terms of the application of incentives, specific incentive parameters can be stated in the solicitation.

#### Non-competitive contracts

Introduce incentives during price negotiations, for application anytime throughout the life of the contract.

### Effective incentive management

An assessment is needed to ensure that incentives are effective in motivating contractor performance, which will vary by contractor characteristics such as:

- ownership
- size
- diversification
- organizational culture

Confirm that the performance objectives can be measured and verified. Consider the extent of control that the contractor has over the achievement of the incentives, and if the contractor’s systems can track the required performance metrics. Assess that the benefits of achieving contract outcomes outweigh the costs of the incentives and their administration to all parties.

### Basis of payment

Incentives can be used with all basis of payment types to reward superior contract performance which exceeds the base standards established in the statement of requirement/work and provides value to Canada.

### Strategic considerations

Keep incentive structures simple with high-level objective measurements rather than complex formulas. Simplify their administration. Any applicable economic price adjustment or foreign currency adjustment must be excluded from incentive calculations.

Engage all relevant stakeholders (such as program, technical, acquisition and price advisors) in the incentive planning process. Modelling of the impact and value of incentive programs to Canada and the contractor is essential. An incentive plan that is not informed by all parties and perspectives can result in unintended consequences on a procurement.

### Incentives types

The types of incentives are available to you are described in the Practitioner’s Guide for Procurement Pricing . They are as follows:

- technical performance incentives
- schedule performance incentives
- award fees

#### Technical performance incentives

Technical performance incentives focus on achieving and enhancing specific technical aspects of a contract that are crucial for adding value to Canada. These incentives aim to encourage excellence and innovation in fulfilling technical requirements. The key elements of technical performance incentives are as follows:

Objective setting : establish clear, measurable and objective technical goals that have to be achieved in performing the contract and bring value to Canada. These goals can be expressed in the form of technical criteria, as well as specifications and requirements.

Incentive structure : design incentives to achieve, enhance or improve technical parameters critical to Canada. When these technical targets are met, the contractor has the opportunity to earn additional profit.

Application : appropriate when improvements or excellence in technical performance provide significant added value or are of critical importance to the procurement and to Canada.

Implementation : begin with the development and definition of performance criteria and parameters related to contract technical specifications. The benefit to Canada of improving the minimum requirement must be clear and definitive.

Performance targets : set three distinct performance targets to structure the incentives, as follows:

- standard performance target
- minimum acceptable performance level
- maximum performance level

Calculation and validation : use variable payments or target incentive fees for calculating the performance incentives. A clear methodology needs to be established for validating the contractor's performance against the set technical performance criteria.
