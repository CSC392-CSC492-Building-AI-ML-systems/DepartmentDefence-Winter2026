## Warranties

In a contract for the sale of goods, any affirmation of fact or any promise by the offeror relating to the goods constitutes an express warranty. The provisions of the standard warranties clause are in addition to, and do not negate or limit, other warranties that are, as a general rule, implied or imposed by law.

### Implied warranties

Two examples of implied warranties are:

- the fitness of goods for the purpose intended
- the merchantable quality of the goods

These warranties are implied in most contracts for the sale of goods under the International Sale of Goods Contracts Convention Act . This act applies across all Canadian provinces and territories, except Quebec. In Quebec, the warranty under the Civil Code is a warranty of ownership and of quality, which includes latent defects.

### Modifications to the standard clause

You may negotiate an increase to the warranty period, if the client agrees to the proposed period and the related costs. The change in warranty period should be detailed in the contract approval document.

Any requests for lessening Canada’s full rights at law, a disclaimer, limitation of the contractor’s liability or decrease of the warranty period must be reviewed by Legal Services, be acceptable to the client and form part of the contract approval document.

### Warranties to cover widespread defects

For contracts involving items with the potential for symptomatic defects or epidemic failures, a broader warranty might be necessary. If several identical items have the same or similar defects, it is prudent to assume these defects may appear across all such items, whether already delivered or pending delivery.

If the client requests this type of warranty or if you deem it advisable, you must, in consultation with the client, determine the scope and nature of the warranty required and request Legal Services to draft a suitable provision. For negotiated firm price contracts, you must obtain the client's agreement on the estimated costs of this warranty.

### Warranty costs

The financial considerations for warranties vary by contract type as follows:

- For firm price competitive contracts , the contractor is responsible for the costs they incur from doing warranty work
- For negotiated firm price contracts, you must:
- consider any contingency for warranty work during price negotiations
- ensure that the amount included in the firm price is reasonable
- provide supporting details as part of the cost summary within the contract approval document
- For negotiated firm price contracts governed by the Defence Production Act , the contractor must certify that the price is based on costs computed in accordance with Contract Cost Principles 1031-2, which do not permit any increase in reserves for guaranteed work; therefore, such warranty costs/expenses must not be included in the contract price since provision for these expenses has already been included in the certified price
- For cost-reimbursable contracts , a contractor cannot include a contingency for warranty as a cost element, although direct costs for labour and materials required under execution of the warranty can be reimbursed, with no overhead or profit permitted

If you believe reasonable warranty costs should be allowed, include an appropriate clause approved by Legal Services in the contract. You should also include a line item for these costs, with or without a maximum estimated expenditure.
