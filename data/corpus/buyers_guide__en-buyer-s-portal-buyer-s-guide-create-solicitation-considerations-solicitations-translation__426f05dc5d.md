## Translation

All notices and documents are given equal treatment under the Official Languages Act , ensuring that translation obligations are uniformly applied.

### Procurement documents requiring translation

Ensure that procurement notices and related tender documents are translated in both official languages before posting them on the Government Electronic Tendering Service (GETS) tender opportunities page.

This includes:

- Notices of proposed procurement (NPP)

- Solicitations of offers, including requests for standing offers (RFSOs) and Requests for supply arrangements (RFSAs)

- Advance contract award notices (ACANs)

- Requests for information (RFI) and Letters of interest (LOI)

- Invitations to qualify (ITQ)

- Invitation to tender (ITT)

- Request for quotation (RFQ)

- Forms, standards, purchase descriptions, statements of work, resulting contracts, blueprints, architectural drawings, reports, graphics, and other documentation included in tender packages

- Additional notices and amendments posted on the GETS tender opportunities page

### Translation services

You and your clients are encouraged to leverage the Translation Bureau for translation, revision, and editing services. For more information, consult Request translation, comparative revision or editing services .

### Translation of documents with professional seals

For documents (e.g. architectural or construction documents and drawings) requiring a professional seal, the client and project authority may identify special translation requirements, which may require expertise in a specific type of document in order to ensure that their translation would be carried out and certified for equal quality in both official languages prior to posting.

With respect to translated documents being of equal quality, provincial and territorial jurisdictions will often have their own stringent provisions for how professionally sealed documents must be translated. Such provisions could include specific professional association standards and/or related legislative or regulatory requirements.

Translation errors, or failure to follow provincial or territorial requirements for translation these documents can pose serious consequences for Canada and other parties (e.g. construction errors, loss of professional license).

### Buyer recommendations to client

You should advise the client to verify the provincial or territorial requirements and seek legal advice when required.

Legal advice may also be sought regarding the legality of translating third-party documents and documents with professional seals, including:

- issues related to intellectual property (e.g. copyright)
- requirements with a final delivery point covered by a comprehensive land claims agreement (CLCA)
- concerns related to contractor liability on documents with professional seals that are translated by third parties

### Special considerations for tendering from pre-qualified offeror lists

In all cases where all offerors are pre-qualified through a two-stage tendering process (e,g, through a supply arrangement) and all have indicated the same (unilingual) language preference, Acquisitions Program (AP ) buyers may provide documents which meet that language preference directly to those offerors on the pre-qualified list.

### Tendering that is national in scope

AP buyers should be aware and take into consideration that the Official Languages Act (OLA) does not make a distinction regarding whether tendering is national in scope where procurement notices and related documents are concerned. All notices or documents must be given the same consideration under the OLA .
