Buy Canadian Procurement Policy Framework
1. Effective date
2. Purpose
The purpose of the Framework is to provide an overarching foundation for Buy Canadian procurement policies to strengthen Canada’s economic resilience and industrial capacity, and support domestic businesses and workers by prioritizing Canadian suppliers, materials and content in federal procurements.
3. Context
The global trade landscape is rapidly changing. The framework of rules governing international trade that provide predictability and a level playing field for Canadian businesses has been disrupted. Canada is among the countries most exposed to the restructuring of global trade. To remain competitive and foster economic resilience, Canada must reinforce its domestic industrial base and mitigate risks associated with supply chain disruptions and trade diversion.
Reinforce domestic supply chains and reduce dependency on foreign inputs in sectors of national importance;
Advance industrial transformation and support regionally inclusive economic development;
Strengthen the participation of Canadian suppliers and the use of Canadian goods and services;
Increase the integration of Canadian-produced materials in federally funded construction and defence procurements;
Prioritize access to procurement opportunities for Canadian firms;
Address regulatory and administrative barriers that limit the ability of Canadian suppliers, including small businesses, to compete effectively in federal procurement processes by streamlining registration, simplifying solicitation requirements, and introducing tailored procurement pathways; and,
Support the growing importance of digital and data sovereignty in federal procurement, including the potential role of Canadian-sourced digital solutions and infrastructure managed under domestic governance.
4. Objectives
The objectives of this Framework are to strengthen Canada’s economic resilience by leveraging federal procurement to prioritize Canadian suppliers, materials, and content.
5. Application
This Framework applies to all departments and agencies as listed in Schedules I, I.1, II of the Financial Administration Act, R.S.C., 1985, c. F-11 .
The procurement is in support of sensitive operations;
The procurement is in support of missions abroad;
The procurement is excluded under section 3(1)(h) of the Government Contracts Regulations, namely contracts entered into with another government or government entity (including requirements fulfilled through the Foreign Military Sales (FMS) program); or,
All of the deliverables under the contract will be solely delivered outside of Canada and lead to significant efficiency gains or savings.
Entities listed under section 5.1 are expected to apply the requirements of this Framework to all procurements.
Notwithstanding section 5.1, all entities of the Government of Canada are strongly encouraged to apply the requirements of the Framework and its accompanying policies.
This Framework and its accompanying policies apply regardless of requirements made in any other directive or policy issued by the Treasury Board, common service provider, or other department or agency related to procurement, including the Directive on the Management of Procurement . While procurement should be conducted in a fair, open and transparent manner, Canadian suppliers, content and materials should be prioritized to the greatest extent allowed by this Framework.
6. Policy Instruments
The following policy instruments and Program are or will be issued under this Framework. Each instrument establishes specific requirements that support the implementation of Canadian prioritization measures in federal procurement.
Mandates the use of Canadian-produced materials (e.g., steel, wood products, and aluminum) in federal construction and defence procurements; and,
Supports integration of materials produced in Canada into design and technical specifications and includes mandatory offeror certification requirements.
Encourages the participation of Canadian suppliers by awarding a reduction to the total value of their financial proposal only for the purpose of evaluating their bids; and;
Incentivizes the use of Canadian content by evaluating bids based on the inclusion of Canadian goods and/or Canadian services or Canadian value-added.
Limits eligibility in non-defence procurements to Canadian goods and services, and those originating from countries that offer reciprocal access under trade agreements; and,
Restricts access to federal procurement opportunities where Canada’s trade agreement procurement obligations do not apply.
Reserves federal procurement opportunities for Canadian small businesses, with mandatory set-asides applied where feasible; and,
Streamlines access to procurement through simplified registration, onboarding, and contracting processes tailored to small business capacity.
7. Roles and Responsibilities
Developing and issuing policy instruments, guidance materials, tools and training to ensure consistent interpretation and application of the various Canadian prioritization measures;
Establishing a strict exceptions process, ensuring that departures from Canadian prioritization measures are invoked only under defined conditions and subject to formal approval as described in each policy instrument;
Coordinating with central agencies and other departments to support alignment with broader economic and industrial policy objectives; and,
Supporting voluntary alignment with the Framework by entities not listed under section 5.1, including through the issuance of guidance materials and tools tailored to procurements involving significant Canadian content or strategic importance.
Supporting government-wide implementation of the Framework through its role in providing strategic direction and guidance, driving awareness and community support; and,
Working with PSPC to ensure alignment of the Framework and Treasury Board policies, including those related to procurement and financial management.
Applying the Framework and the relevant policy instruments issued under this Framework in accordance with their scope, thresholds, and conditions;
Ensuring procurement planning, documentation, and execution reflect the obligations set out in the Framework and applicable policy instruments;
Invoking exceptions only in accordance with the conditions and approval processes defined in supporting instruments, and ensuring such cases remain exceptional and justified;
Maintaining complete and accurate records to demonstrate compliance with policy instruments and any exceptions invoked; and,
Performing evaluations, audits, and reviews related to the implementation of this Framework and associated policies.
8. Authorities
This Framework is issued under the authority of the Minister of Public Works and Government Services pursuant to the Department of Public Works and Government Services Act and the Defence Production Act which provides the Minister with authority over the procurement of goods, services, construction, and defence supplies.
Government-wide implementation is supported by the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, consistent with its authority pursuant to the Financial Administration Act to establish administrative policies applicable to departments and agencies.
The One Canadian Economy Act , which provides a legislative framework to strengthen domestic economic resilience and support projects of national interest;
The Government Contracts Regulations ( SOR /87-402), which set out conditions for entering into contracts on behalf of the Crown;
The Policy on the Planning and Management of Investments ; and,
The Directive on the Management of Procurement .
9. Requirements
Integrate applicable Canadian prioritization requirements into procurement planning, including Statements of Work, technical specifications, and evaluation criteria, in accordance with the scope and conditions of each supporting policy instrument;
Apply the measures prescribed in each policy instrument consistently and concurrently, as required;
Ensure that offeror certifications, declarations or attestations are obtained where required, following policy requirements defined in supporting instruments;
Invoke exceptions only in accordance with the conditions and approval processes defined in supporting policy instruments;
Ensure that Canadian prioritization measures are reflected in Statements of Work, technical specifications, and evaluation criteria. Where a procurement is conducted by a common service provider (e.g., PSPC , SSC ) on their behalf, departments and agencies must provide the common service provider with sufficient information to enable appropriate application of the Framework.
Consider whether Canadian capability has changed since the original award where contract options are exercised with non-Canadian suppliers. Although options are governed by existing terms and conditions, departments should assess whether re-procurement would better advance Canadian preference objectives, particularly where new domestic capacity has emerged; and,
Where a procurement may fall within the scope of both the Policy on Prioritizing Canadian Materials in Federal Procurement and the Policy on Prioritizing Canadian Suppliers and Canadian Content in Strategic Federal Procurement , departments and agencies shall apply both policies concurrently unless an exception has been approved.
10. Monitoring, Oversight, and Reporting
Ensure documentation is sufficient to demonstrate compliance with applicable Canadian prioritization measures;
Make procurement records available for audit, review, and performance assessment; and,
Cooperate with any government-wide evaluations or reviews related to the implementation of this Framework.
Comply with the reporting obligations set out in each policy instrument issued under this Framework, including any requirements related to frequency, format, and performance indicators;
Ensure reporting is supported by complete and accurate documentation, including certifications, exceptions, attestations and compliance records where applicable;
Apply any guidance issued by PSPC to support consistent reporting and performance tracking across instruments; and,
Support any government-wide data reporting requirements.
