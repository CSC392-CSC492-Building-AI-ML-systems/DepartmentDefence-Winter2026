# Conflict and hierarchy stress-test prompts.
# Goal: force retrieval/answering across Buyer’s Guide + Buy Canadian + TBS directive.
# See docs/EVALUATION.md for how to use and interpret this set.
If the Buy Canadian Procurement Policy Framework says its policies apply regardless of other Treasury Board directives, but the TBS Directive on the Management of Procurement says it prevails over other departmental procurement instruments in case of conflict, which rule should govern and what must be documented in the file?
For a procurement that appears covered by both trade agreements and a modern treaty (CLCA) right of first refusal, which obligation takes precedence if they conflict, and does that remove the need to solicit bids from designated beneficiaries?
A strategic non-defence procurement appears to be in scope for both the Interim Policy on Reciprocal Procurement and the newer Policy on Prioritizing Canadian Suppliers and Canadian Content. Which policy should be applied when both seem applicable, and what happens to suppliers from non-trading partner countries?
During an emergency, can a contracting authority skip normal bid solicitation and approvals entirely, or do emergency-specific approval conditions and post-award reporting still apply under the TBS directive?
A supplier from a non-trading partner country bids on a requirement where the strategic Buy Canadian supplier/content policy applies with no approved exception. Is that supplier eligible, and how does this interact with reciprocal procurement language in older guidance?
If an exception is invoked under Buy Canadian policy by a common service provider on behalf of multiple departments, whose approval is required and what supporting rationale must be kept?
If a Buyer’s Guide workflow note appears to conflict with a TBS directive requirement about contracting approvals, which is higher-order, and how should a contracting authority justify the final decision path?
Can a public interest exception that permits awarding to an ineligible/suspended supplier under integrity rules override unrelated supplier-eligibility restrictions under reciprocal or Buy Canadian policies, or must each policy’s conditions still be satisfied separately?
