Fixed price
Fixed price contracts provide price certainty for the duration of the contract, ensuring the contractor is paid a definite sum of money to perform the work regardless of the costs incurred. This basis of payment incentivizes the contractor to control costs efficiently, as they are responsible for all costs under or over the fixed price. Furthermore, the administrative burden on both contracting parties is minimized.
See the section 4.1.1 of the Practitioner’s Guide for Procurement Pricing for more guidance on the decision process, procedures and examples for use of this basis of payment type.
Options
There are three options for the fixed price basis of payment:
fixed price competitive, where the price is established through market competition involving more than one compliant offeror, ensuring the price reflects fair market value
fixed price non-competitive, where the price is negotiated before a contract or contract amendment is issued
fixed price with economic price adjustment, which provides the ability to adjust for significant price fluctuations outside of Canada and the contractor’s control
Appropriate use
Use the fixed price basis of payment when:
a competitive process with multiple compliant offerors
benchmarking and should-cost information to validate the price
robust historical data to validate prices before fixing them; for example, to establish a fixed price for a follow-on contract, assurance services can be performed on costs and profit of the previous contract
the requirements, scope and outputs determine the level of effort are well-defined; stable; and are unlikely to change for example, where the technology, industry, platform or service is mature or proven
the contractor does not need to include additional profit premiums to account for long-term uncertainties in requirements or costs
you have a reasonable level of assurance that the price will still be reasonable and of value to Canada in the later years of the contract given the potential for technology changes and process improvements
the schedule is relatively certain and achievable
it is appropriate for the contractor to bear the risk of cost uncertainty
Limitations
A fixed price basis of payment may not be suitable for high risk procurements with highly uncertain or variable scopes. For example, for procurements that involve innovative or untested technology.
Audits
Fixed price contracts do not provide for discretionary audits to reassess profits or costs during the term of the contract or upon completion. However, you must ensure that Canada retains its audit rights for information purposes for possible future negotiations on interim or follow-on contracts. This should include the right to audit the underlying costs, including production costs, to provide the necessary level of price support to determine whether the fixed price is fair and reasonable.
