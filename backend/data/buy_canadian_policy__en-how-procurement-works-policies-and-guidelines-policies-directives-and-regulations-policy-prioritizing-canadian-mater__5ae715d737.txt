Policy on Prioritizing Canadian Materials in Federal Procurement
1. Effective date
The Policy does not apply to solicitations published, or contracts awarded prior to the effective date.
For existing Supply Arrangements (SAs), including any solicitations issued from an existing SA , the Policy applies at the time of the next renewal. Departments may choose to incorporate Materials requirements earlier, provided these elements are reflected in the updated Request for Supply Arrangement (RFSA). For existing Standing Offers (SOs), the Policy applies at their next renewal.
2. Purpose
To protect and support domestic industry, Canada is increasing the integration of materials produced in Canada, reducing dependency on foreign inputs and requiring companies contracting with the federal government to source materials produced in Canada for use in their supply chains.
3. Definitions
A procurement as set out in Appendix A .
A good as set out in Appendix B .
The materials (steel, wood products, and aluminum) and their corresponding definitions set out in Appendix C .
Materials that have undergone primary manufacturing and fabrication in Canada.
4. Application
The Policy is issued pursuant to the Buy Canadian Procurement Policy Framework .
Subject to section 5.2 of the Buy Canadian Procurement Policy Framework, the Policy applies to all competitive and non-competitive defence goods procurements and construction procurements by or on behalf of any federal departments or agencies valued at $25 million or greater inclusive of all applicable taxes.
For procurements above this threshold, the Policy requirements regarding Materials apply individually to each material where the performance of the contract is expected to require the use of that material in an amount equal to or greater than the dollar value threshold set out for that material as set out in Appendix C .
Canada reserves the right to apply the requirements of the Policy to procurements that are otherwise excluded from application, at its sole discretion. Departments and agencies are strongly encouraged to apply the requirements of the Policy to excluded procurements wherever feasible where the procurement involves significant use of Materials, even below applicable thresholds, to support domestic industry.
Best value – where the use of Materials produced in Canada is expected to result in an overall cost increase of 25% or more over using non-Canadian materials, provided that the cost differential is substantiated through documented market analysis;
Public Interest – where the application of the Policy would otherwise be inconsistent with the public interest;
No capacity or availability – where the materials are not produced in Canada in sufficient and reasonably available quantities and/or of a satisfactory quality;
Undue Delay – where the application of the Policy would result in delays to the procurement of critical defence or national security equipment, which could negatively impact Canadian defence or national security operations;
Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS) and Military Off-The-Shelf (MOTS) Products.
Where the procurement is conducted by a common service provider (e.g., Public Services and Procurement Canada, Shared Services Canada), the Minister responsible for the common service provider must also approve the invocation of any exception prior to solicitation. This requirement applies regardless of whether the exception is initiated by the business owner.
For procurements that are issued by a common service provider on behalf of multiple departments or as part of a government-wide procurement instrument (e.g., National Master Standing Offers), the Minister responsible for the common service provider may approve the use of exceptions under section 4.5 without requiring approval from individual business owners. In such cases, the common service provider must ensure that the rationale and supporting documentation reflect the scope and impact of the exception across all affected departments.
5. Policy Requirements
Determine if the Policy applies to the procurement in question, pursuant to sections 4.2, 4.3, and 4.4.;
Where an exception is determined to apply, the business owner and/or common service provider shall obtain written approval from their Minister, in accordance with section 4.6, prior to the issuance of any solicitation;
The use of each material defined in Appendix C of the Policy is mandatory in the performance of the Contract when the total estimated value for the material meets or exceeds the applicable thresholds set out in Appendix C ;
A mandatory certification as specified in Appendix D that must be completed by all offerors, under which they commit to use Materials in the performance of the contract; and,
Clauses that require suppliers to maintain records regarding the origin of Materials under the certification and to provide such records to Canada upon request.
Evaluate each offer and request the mandatory certification from offerors if it isn’t included in their offer. Disqualify as non-compliant any offer from a offeror that does not include or submit the required mandatory certification.
Where a contractor fails to comply with its contractual obligations related to Materials, take appropriate measures to ensure contractor compliance. This may include applying liquidated damages, retaining holdbacks, terminating the contract for default, and/or disqualifying the contractor from future procurements.
After contract award, monitor performance of the contract. If there is any reason to doubt a contractor’s compliance with the contractual obligations related to Materials, request records regarding their origin.
Approval of any such request is subject to the authorization of the business owner’s Minister, and, where the procurement is conducted by a common service provider (e.g. Public Services and Procurement Canada, Shared Services Canada), the minister responsible for the common service provider, as stipulated in section 4.6 of this Policy.
Departments must ensure that contractors continue to meet all contractual obligations including delivery, quality, and performance requirements, while approval is pending. Approval delays do not relieve the contractor of these obligations. If the request is approved, departments must honour valid claims for payment in accordance with the amended terms.
The contracting authority may determine that a price adjustment is warranted to offset the difference in Canadian-produced Material value. Any such adjustment must be reasonable and informed by sufficient documentation. In the absence of sufficient documentation provided by the Contractor, the decision to adjust pricing or contract terms will be at the sole discretion of the contracting authority.
This may include monitoring market conditions, supplier capacity, and fabrication capabilities to determine whether Materials produced in Canada have become reasonably available in sufficient quantity and quality.
Availability must also be reassessed prior to exercising any contract options. Departments and agencies must assess whether Materials produced in Canada have become available.
Where feasible, the mandatory use of such Materials should be scoped back into the contract, with consideration of any necessary adjustments to scope, schedule, or price, where justified.
Ensure that accurate and complete procurement records are created and maintained in the procurement file.
Approved exceptions must be fully documented by the contracting authority in the procurement file, including the rationale, supporting evidence, and required approvals.
6. Roles and Responsibilities
Developing and issuing supporting policy instruments, guidance materials, and tools to ensure consistent interpretation and application of the Policy.
Implementing mechanisms to monitor compliance, track performance, and assess the effectiveness of the various requirements regarding Materials produced in Canada.
Determining applicability of the Policy to each procurement;
Ensuring statements of work and specifications support Materials produced in Canada; and
Obtaining and validating offeror certifications;
7. Monitoring and Reporting
Monitor compliance with the Policy throughout the procurement lifecycle;
Ensure that accurate and complete procurement records are created and maintained to support verification of compliance with the Policy, including offeror certifications, post-award verifications, and any approved exceptions where applicable;
Support any government-wide data reporting requirements; and
Maintain records of offeror certifications, post-award compliance checks, and any deviations from contractual obligations.
8. Enquiries
Please direct enquiries about this Policy to the organizational unit in your department responsible for this subject matter. For interpretation of the Policy, the responsible organizational unit should contact: spac.paachetezcanadien-apbuycanadian.pspc@tpsgc-pwgsc.gc.ca .

Appendix C: Materials
This appendix outlines the definitions and thresholds for Materials produced in Canada required under the Policy. Sub-appendices are provided for each type of Material.
C1: Canadian Steel
Canadian steel means:
Steel that is melted and poured (i.e., created from the initial transformation of raw materials) in Canada and the finished products made from that steel.
Steel melted and poured in Canada means:
The raw steel, or any portion of the raw steel, contained in the material first produced in a liquid state in a steel-making furnace and poured into its first solid state in Canada. Solid state can take the form of a semi-finished or finished steel mill product.
[ Threshold : $250,000]

C3: Canadian Aluminium
Canadian aluminum means:
Aluminum that is smelted and cast in Canada and the finished products made from that aluminum.
Smelted and cast in Canada means:
for all aluminum material except aluminum sheets and plates: the largest volume of primary aluminum contained in the material was produced in Canada; and
for all aluminum sheets and plates: any percentage of primary aluminum contained in the material was produced in Canada.
For the purposes of the Policy, Canadian aluminum shall be interpreted to include products classified under the following 6-digit Harmonized System (HS) codes, provided they meet the definition of aluminum where the country of smelt and cast (CSC) is Canada as defined above.
