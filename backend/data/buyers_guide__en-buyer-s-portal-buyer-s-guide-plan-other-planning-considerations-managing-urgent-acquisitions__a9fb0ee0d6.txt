Managing urgent aquisitions
An urgent requirement is one that needs immediate action. Defining a requirement as urgent depends on the priorities of the client and government. Urgency might be based on avoiding significant economic impact, protecting health and safety programs, or meeting key project deadlines. The buyer will decide, in consultation with the client and PSPC management as required, if the requirement is to be treated as an urgent acquisition. Early engagement between PSPC and the client is important to develop a procurement strategy that meets the client’s operational objectives.
To effectively manage a requirement that has been identified as an urgent acquisition:
form an integrated team of dedicated personnel from PSPC and the client
consider assigning a single point of contact in each organization when there are multiple urgent requirements
set up clear communication channels between all stakeholders early on
prioritize the requirement and apply concurrent review processes where possible
Request for Information (RFI)
Solicitation of Interest and Qualification (SOIQ)
Price and Availability Enquiry (P&A)
If Treasury Board (TB) approval is required but hasn’t been obtained by the client, consider preparing a joint submission signed by both ministers to seek advance contract approval at the same time. You should consult the Treasury Board Secretariat Program Sector analyst while drafting this submission to get advice and guidance on the content and approval process.
